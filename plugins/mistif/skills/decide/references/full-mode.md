# Full mode and extra lenses

## More lenses
Pick the ones that suit the decision; five or six is plenty.
| Lens | It checks | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| money | unit economics, cash timing, the cost of being wrong | there is a price or a budget |
| new customer | would a stranger understand and pay | selling something |
| competitor | what they do if this works | there is a market |
| engineer | what is hard or slow to build | building something |
| statistician | is the evidence enough to conclude anything | numbers are given |
| small-business owner | time, cash, and a Monday morning reality | a small operator is involved |
| lawyer | rules, contracts, liability | regulated or contractual territory |
| investor | upside, downside, what would make it worth more | funding or a big commitment |

## Advisor brief (full mode)
Give each advisor: the standalone framing; its lens and what that lens looks for; and these instructions.
- Work alone. You cannot see the other advisors.
- Commit to your angle and argue it.
- Quote the line or assumption each point rests on.
- Do not invent numbers. Name any figure you would need.
- Under 250 words.

## Challenge brief
Give each advisor the other advisors' answers. Ask for the strongest objection to each, quoting the exact text it attacks, in under 100 words per advisor. Drop any objection that does not quote.

## A good cheapest-test line
"Show the 60-second demo to 20 people in the target group this week with a waitlist link. Pass: at least 4 sign up with an email. Stop: fewer than 2, or nobody can say what it does back to you."
