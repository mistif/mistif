# Shape lenses

Use these to get different options, not more options. Pick four or five and run them.

## Ways to vary an idea
- **Flip it**: do the opposite, or remove the thing everyone assumes is needed.
- **Drop a limit**: what would you build with unlimited time, or with one afternoon?
- **Change the user**: the same job for a beginner, an expert, a team, a machine.
- **Merge or split**: fold two features into one, or cut one into two products.
- **Shrink**: the version a single person could use today, by hand if need be.
- **Borrow**: how does another field solve the same shape of problem?
- **Scale up**: what breaks at ten times the volume, and is that the real product?

## Judging directions
Score each of the two or three survivors on three questions, in words rather than numbers:
1. Does it solve a real problem for the named user?
2. Can we build and check it with what we have?
3. Is it different enough from what already exists to be worth building?

## Kinds of assumption
Write each as a sentence that could be wrong, with a cheap test next to it.
- Demand: people want this.
- Behaviour: they will change what they do.
- Feasibility: the data, API or skill exists.
- Cost: it is affordable at the size that matters.
- Fit: it works inside the current system and rules.

## First scope
One user, one job, the riskiest assumption tested. Anything else goes on the cut-list with a reason, so it is a decision and not an omission.
